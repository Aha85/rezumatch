# Writing quality: human, clear, no AI tells

Run this on every draft before the user sees it: resume, cover letter, short notes.

## Every line
- One idea per bullet. No semicolon chains.
- Holds a name, a number or a specific moment. If it could appear in anyone's resume, rewrite or cut it.
- No slogans, no X-versus-Y punchlines ("Not a tool demo. A strategy."), no aphorisms nobody could disagree with.
- No keyword walls. Each keyword once, inside a true sentence.
- No word repeated within two sentences (watch: write, build, AI, team, agents).
- No em dashes. Use a comma, a period or a colon.
- No stock AI words: leverage, spearhead, synergy, cutting-edge, passionate, dynamic, results-driven, seamless, robust, delve, tapestry, testament.

## Paragraphs (letters and summaries)
- Read it out loud. The order follows how a person would tell it: what you made, then how you made it.
- Sentences are linked with connectors (so, that way, which means, because, beyond that), never stacked as separate claims.
- Every pronoun (they, it, this) points to one clear noun in the same or previous sentence. If not, repeat the noun.
- At most one paragraph opens with "I".
- No sudden jumps between topics. If a paragraph covers two themes, split it.

## Sounding like a person
- Contractions in letters (that's, I'd, don't).
- Generic greetings in lowercase after the company: "Dear Acme hiring team,".
- Numbers as digits, phrased as spoken: "more than 8 people", "$50,000-a-year", not "8+" or "/year" in a letter.
- Bold the 3-5 numbers the reader must not miss. Emphasis is a human choice.
- Honest limits as plain facts, never as a confession.

## Keep the keywords
- The writing pass must not drop listing keywords. Rerun ats_score.py after each rewrite and put back any term that fell out, in a true sentence.

## The user's own words win
- When the user rewrites a line, keep their version. Flag only a real error (fact, grammar that changes meaning, a broken link).
