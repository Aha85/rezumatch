# Recruiter and HR guidelines

## How a resume is read
- First pass is a fast scan: name, current title, companies, years, the first lines of the top role. Put the strongest proof there.
- Recruiters check the resume against LinkedIn. Titles and dates must match.
- Hiring managers read for proof, not adjectives: a name, a number, a moment.

## Titles and seniority
- Keep the official title. Background checks and reference calls compare it.
- Real scope above the title: add the level in parentheses and prove it in the first bullet.
  "Head of Customer Support (Director level)" + "Ran a department of 12, reporting to the CEO."
- A new title the user confirms for their own business is fine ("Marketing Consultant"). Warn if their LinkedIn says something that contradicts it.
- Explain levels when asked. Director: owns a whole area, sets strategy, owns goals or budget, leads a team, reports to senior management. Head of / Lead / Manager vary by company size.

## What to keep and cut
- Default: the last ~10 years, roles relevant to the listing. Older or unrelated roles go, or collapse into one line.
- Short roles (under ~6 months) with nothing relevant can go.
- A small related role folds into another as one bullet, starting with "Also" when it's a different organization.
- Personal projects: keep when they prove a skill the listing asks for. Link the portfolio.
- No photo, age, ID number, marital status (Israel included), unless the user insists.

## Honesty lines that protect the user
- Never inflate scope, title, team size or numbers. One lie found in an interview ends the process.
- Limits stated as facts, without apology: "I haven't managed people directly; I led a 5-person project team for 8 months."
- Gaps the resume can't close honestly (missing domain, missing title) are handled in the cover letter or in the interview.

## Cover letters
- 150-250 words for a text box; up to one page as an upload.
- Paragraph themes in order, each linked to the one before: why this role, proof, how you work, earlier experience, close.
- Close with an approach, not a ready solution: the candidate doesn't know the company's internals.
- Name only employers that appear in the resume.

## After submitting
- Fill optional fields (LinkedIn "top choice", questions). Use the user's own reason, in their words.
- Align LinkedIn: title, headline, summary lines that contradict the resume.
- A short note to the job poster when a name is visible.
